A robust drift contact for the P2 window

Today the mylar window gets its 800 V through copper tape and a soldered wire. This page shows where that wire runs, why the bare mesh limits where a contact can go, and the option that fixes it with one drilled hole.

Do thisA. Drill a Ø1.2 mm hole from the mylar ledge into the existing wire slot. Bring the wire up to a copper tab under the window and pot the slot. No PCB or mask change.
Add laterA2. A second slot through the bottom wall at x = 575 mm, for a redundant feed.
FallbackB. A drift pad on the PCB with a spring. It only meets the 1 kV clearance at the two inner corners and needs a PCB re-spin.
SkipD. Gas through a hole in the PCB. The frame already distributes gas, and the hole causes trouble in lamination.

1 · How the edge of the detector is built

This is a real cut through the frame across the bottom straight edge, drawn to scale. The PCB, resist and mesh are thinner than a line at this scale, so they are drawn thicker.

Three potentials meet here. The window's coating is at about 800 V (red). The mesh is at about 450 V (blue), and the readout pads are near ground. Where the mesh sits inside the resist, it is insulated: 75 µm of cured Dynamask holds about 5 kV. Over the active area the mesh is bare, and so is the gold pad where the mesh is contacted. Every bare drift-potential part must stay at least 3 mm from bare mesh, which is the clearance for 1 kV.

The active edge is only 4.8 mm from the frame wall in V3B_final, and it must not move. This is why a contact on the PCB, standing up next to the wall, has trouble meeting the 3 mm rule (section 5). A contact up at the window, inside the frame, does not.

3 · Concept A: contact inside the glue joint

The existing wire slot runs through the outer wall directly under the mylar ledge. Only 0.2 mm of frame separates the slot from the ledge. One small hole connects them, so the wire can come up and meet the window's coating inside the glue joint.

How to build it

  1. Drill Ø1.2 mm vertically from the ledge into the slot at (601.5, 3.55), through the 0.2 mm roof. Deburr.
  2. Feed the HV wire through the slot from outside, as today. Strip it so that only bare conductor goes up the hole; the insulation ends inside the slot.
  3. Solder the conductor to a copper foil tab, 2 × 6 mm, 35–50 µm thick, laid along the ledge. Fill the hole with room-temperature silver epoxy. Keep the tab at most 2 mm wide so that at least 1 mm of the 3 mm glue land stays continuous.
  4. Glue the mylar as usual, with a dab of silver epoxy on the tab only. The coated side faces down onto the tab.
  5. Fill the slot with epoxy from outside. This buries the wire and closes the leak path.
  6. Solder the wire to the D pad, as today.
  7. Check: resistance from the D pad to the window surface, a 1 kV drift-to-mesh hold test before closing, and a leak test.

Keep every cure at 60 °C or below until the frame material's limit is known. Concept A only works if the window's conductive coating faces the PCB.

4 · A2: a second, redundant feed

Concept A still has one contact per detector. A second one has to go through the frame wall somewhere it does not cut the gas system and stays well away from bare mesh.

On existing frames, a Ø1.2 mm horizontal hole at z = 3.2 mm does the same job as the new slot. Along the bottom edge the frame is flush with the board, so the wire runs along the frame's outer face to the same D pad.

5 · B: drift pad on the PCB with a spring

A Ø2 mm gold pad on the PCB, next to the wall, with a 4 mm spring or pogo pin pushing up onto the window. The pad is bare drift potential at board level, right next to the bare mesh. With the active edge only 4.8 mm from the wall, there is not enough room for the 3 mm rule along most of the frame.

B stays the fallback if A gives trouble. It costs a PCB re-spin and an extra process step, and its resist window can be generated from the V3B_final config once a pad position is chosen.

6 · Other ideas

C · Conductive elastomer instead of the spring

Gentler on the window, but silicone outgassing is an ageing risk in gas detectors. Only with a low-outgassing grade, and only together with B.

D · Gas through a hole in the PCB

Not recommended. The frame already feeds gas through 2 ports and 26 nozzles. In the bulk process, Dynamask tents the hole on the carrier plate, the mesh lies over it, and developer or rinse water gets trapped. Putting a gas hole and an HV contact in the same place also creates a leak path and a creepage path together. If a dead flow region shows up, add a nozzle to the frame instead.

7 · Masks, and what is still open

A and A2 need no mask change. V3B_final is used as it is, whatever pillar diameter is chosen after the test. It keeps {{act_v3b}} cm² of active area, against {{act_v2}} cm² with V2.
