Mesh and drift contacts in 3D

This shows both HV contacts as they would be with concept B: where each voltage enters the board, how it gets inside the frame, and where it touches the mesh or the window. The frame is the real STL, the copper is the real PCB Gerbers and the resist is the V3B_final M2 mask. The new parts are the proposal. Everything is to scale in mm, so use Explode to pull the thin layers apart.

Mesh, 450 V

  1. Cable soldered to the M pad, outside the frame at the bottom-right corner.
  2. Front-copper trace under the frame wall and the O-ring.
  3. Gold mesh-contact pad inside the frame. The mesh is laminated straight onto it through a window in M2.

Drift, 800 V

  1. Cable soldered to the D pad, right next to M.
  2. Via in the D pad down to a back-copper trace, about {{routelen}} mm under the frame band to the inner-left corner.
  3. Via just outside the O-ring, then a short front-copper trace under the frame wall.
  4. Gold drift pad on the PCB, with a soldered pogo pin up to a copper tab glued on the window's aluminium coating.

    Two variants of B

    Square pad in B2. The drift pad is 3 × 3 mm. The mesh is laminated last and trimmed at the end, so the cut edge stays bare. It is cut as a 10 × 10 mm square, 3.5 mm from the pad edge everywhere, which leaves 0.5 mm of margin for hand cutting. The resist island (about 10.8 × 11.6 mm) extends about 1 mm beyond the cut on the open sides, so the mesh edge rests on resist. Its bottom and right sides merge with the existing resist border in the corner. The red ring is the 3 mm keep-out from the pad edge, a square with rounded corners, and it stays inside the cut.

    What the model shows

    Both cables land at the same place. At the bottom-right corner the M and D pads sit side by side, as they do today, so the HV cabling doesn't change. What changes is how the drift voltage reaches the inside of the frame: today a wire goes through the frame slot, and with B it runs through the board on the back copper. The wire slot is no longer needed and can be filled.

    The two contacts use the same crossing under the frame. The mesh trace already runs on the front copper under the frame wall and the O-ring. The drift trace does the same at the inner-left corner. The only difference is the via just outside the O-ring: the board has no room outside the frame at that corner, so the drift voltage has to come along the back. Because the via is outside the O-ring, it is outside the gas volume and needs no plugging.

    The two contacts touch their electrodes differently. The mesh is laminated straight onto its gold pad during the bulk process. The window is 4 mm above the board, so the drift pad needs a pogo pin, soldered after the bulk is cured.

    Inner-left is the only corner that works. The pad edge is {{clear}} mm from bare mesh. At the inner-bottom corner, V3B_final's evacuation channel leaves bare mesh exactly where the pad would go.

    Mesh edge. The mesh stops at the outer edge of the resist border, about 1.9 mm from the frame wall. At the inner-left corner the drift pad overlaps that edge, so the mesh gets a Ø8 mm hole around the pad, trimmed at the end like the rest of the mesh. That keeps every piece of mesh 3 mm from the pad edge. The hole still ends 0.5 mm short of the active area.

    No readout pad is lost. The drift pad sits in the dead band between the frame wall and the active edge. The nearest readout pad is 3.8 mm from its edge.

    The top corner cannot take the drift pad. That corner is already used by the top mesh-contact pad: the existing gold pad is only 1.3 mm from the active edge and 1.6 mm from the nearest readout pad. Even with that pad left unused, a new drift pad there would sit next to bare mesh.
