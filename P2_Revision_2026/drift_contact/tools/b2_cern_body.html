Drift contact on the PCB: the B2 proposal

Today the mylar window gets its drift voltage from a wire that runs through a slot in the drift frame, soldered to copper tape stuck on the window. It works, but the tape's adhesive outgasses, and the bare wire leaves the slot only 2.8 mm from the bare mesh-contact pad, below the 3 mm we want for 1 kV.

B2 moves the drift contact onto the PCB. We give up one readout pad, the corner pad /Sector0/Sig1 next to the HV pads, and put a gold drift pad there. A pogo pin soldered on it presses on the window's aluminium coating. Both HV cables stay where they are today, on M and D.

Mesh, ≈450 V (unchanged)

  1. Cable soldered to the M pad, outside the frame.
  2. Front-copper trace under the frame wall and the O-ring.
  3. Gold mesh-contact pad. The mesh lies on it through a window in M2.

Drift, ≈800 V (new)

  1. Cable soldered to the D pad, next to M, as today.
  2. Via in the D pad, then a {{b2len}} mm back-copper trace in a gap cut through the GND hatch.
  3. Via next to the new pad, then a short front-copper trace.
  4. 3 × 3 mm gold drift pad and a soldered pogo pin, whose tip lands on a copper tab glued to the window coating.
